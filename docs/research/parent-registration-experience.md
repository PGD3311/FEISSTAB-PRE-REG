# Parent Registration Experience: Comprehensive Research Report

_Research conducted 2026-03-19_

---

## 1. The Current Registration Flow — Step by Step

### Platform Landscape

There is no single registration system. Parents must create and maintain accounts across **multiple independent platforms**, each serving different feiseanna by region or organizer preference:

| Platform | Region/Focus | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| **FeisWeb** | Northeastern US | Feis entries, tabulation, results. Run by Alan (alan@feisweb.com). Family-oriented account model. |
| **FeisWorx** | Western US, Mid-America, Southern, Western Canada | Family-owned business. PO Box in Parker, CO. info@feisworx.com. Oldest/most established. |
| **QuickFeis** | Southern US, Mid-America, national events (NAIDC) | Detroit-based. Has a mobile app. Live stage schedule feature. Used for Oireachtas events. |
| **iFeis** | Various (ifeis.net) | Requires Google or Facebook login. Designed for Chrome. Not IE/Edge compatible. |
| **FeisFWD** | Growing adoption, CLRG approved | Newest platform. Stripe + PayPal payments. Auto email confirmations. Video upload support. |
| **FeisEntry** | Various (feisentry.com) | Separate parent login portal. |
| **GoFeis** | International — CLRG, WIDA, CRDM, other orgs | Free to set up, charges per dancer entry. |
| **Feis.link** | Various | Online registration and tabulation. |

**Key pain point: A family attending feiseanna in different regions may need accounts on 3-4 different platforms.** Each platform has different UX, different fields, different payment systems. Dancer profiles must be created separately on each. There is no data portability between them.

### The Step-by-Step Registration Flow

**Pre-registration (teacher-guided):**
1. Teacher tells parent which feis to attend
2. Teacher tells parent which dances (Reel, Light Jig, Slip Jig, Treble Jig, Hornpipe) and which level for each dance
3. Parent looks up the feis syllabus to find competition numbers matching their dancer's age group + level + dance type

**Account creation (one-time per platform):**
1. Go to platform website (e.g., feisworx.com)
2. Create a registrant account (parent/guardian) — email, password, mailing address
3. Add dancer profiles — each child needs: full name, date of birth, gender, dance school, championship status
4. On FeisWorx: the system uses birthday, championship status, and gender to filter which competitions are shown
5. On QuickFeis: dancer must ensure name spelling, DOB, gender, and school are correct — teachers cannot edit dancer accounts
6. On iFeis: must use Google or Facebook OAuth — no standalone account

**Registering for a specific feis:**
1. Find the feis in the platform's list of upcoming events
2. Select the dancer from your family account
3. Browse filtered competition list — the system shows age-eligible and level-eligible options based on the dancer's profile
4. Select each competition (dance) to register for — typically 2-5 dances depending on level
5. For championship levels: may need to specify set dance selection
6. Review cart — see per-dance fees and family fee
7. Pay — method depends on feis/platform: credit card, PayPal, or in some cases **print and mail a check**
8. Receive confirmation (FeisFWD sends automatic emails; others vary)

**FeisWorx four-step process (stated on their homepage):**
1. Create a Registrant (parent/guardian/school registrar)
2. Add Competitors (Dancers/Musicians)
3. Select a Competitor / Feis combination
4. Print and mail registration form with payment (or pay online, if available)

Note: FeisWorx **still lists "print and mail" as the primary payment method** with online payment as secondary — showing how recently this world moved to digital.

## 2. Pain Points Parents Experience

### Registration Complexity

- **Multiple accounts across platforms.** Dance schools recommend parents "sign up for accounts on commonly used sites including quickfeis.com, feisweb.com, feisworx.com, and efeis.com" — four separate accounts before you even register for one feis.

- **Teacher dependency.** Parents cannot independently determine which competitions to enter. They MUST ask their teacher first. One school writes: "Your teacher will tell you which dances and what level your dancer should register for." This creates a bottleneck — if the teacher is slow to respond, registration can close.

- **Per-dance-per-level confusion.** A dancer can be at different levels for different dances simultaneously (e.g., Novice for Reel but Advanced Beginner for Slip Jig). Parents must track placements from past feiseanna to know current eligibility for each individual dance. Schools acknowledge: "Each dance can be in a different level so dancers can be in MULTIPLE levels at one time!"

- **Capacity limits and early closures.** "All feiseanna have a cap/limit and will cause the feis to close entries earlier than the entry deadline." Parents who hesitate or wait for teacher guidance can miss out entirely.

- **Payment friction.** FeisWorx: credit card acceptance is per-feis (some feiseanna opt out of online payment entirely). Some require PayPal. Some still require mailing a physical check. One documented case: a parent couldn't pay on FeisFWD for a state competition.

- **Competitor number printing.** If you have an outstanding payment, "you won't be able to print your card from home — you'll have to go to the registration table at the feis, pay your outstanding amount and have registration print your card."

- **Late fees.** Entries after the initial deadline: typically $25/dancer, escalating to $50. Changes after deadline: one-time change fee ($5+) plus difference in competition fees. No changes allowed day-of.

- **Non-refundable fees.** "Feis fees are non-refundable once paid."

- **Obsolete technology.** FeisWorx homepage is raw HTML with table-based layouts. iFeis does not work on Internet Explorer or Edge. These are not modern mobile-first experiences.

### Day-of Frustrations

- **Long waits with unpredictable schedules.** "A dancer's first dance was at 10:30am and didn't dance again until 2pm, leaving her tired and frustrated from waiting so long." Parents describe feiseanna as "a very, very, long, long day."

- **Getting lost.** "The getting to the feis part can be very frustrating because of the extra traffic and trying to find your way to a place that might be obscure."

- **No supervision.** "Dancers do not move as a group at the feis and are not under the supervision of the Dance Teacher." Parents must be the "sport manager" (aka "Feis Mom" or "Feis Dad").

- **Bore factor for siblings.** Schools advise: "Bring something for non-dancing kids to do while they wait" and "consider headphones or hearing protection for everyone since feis can be loud."

### Registration Error Risk

- **It is the competitor's and teacher's responsibility to enter correct age and level.** "It is NOT the responsibility of the Feis entry committee to carefully review each application for correctness."
- If entered in the wrong competition and the correct competition has already danced, "the competition does not need to be reopened."
- **No day-of changes.** "Entrants cannot change competitions, categories or dances on the day of the competition."

## 3. The Eligibility / Level System (Parent Perspective)

### The Progression Ladder

| Level | Who | How to Advance | Dances |
|-------|-----|---------------|--------|
| **First Feis** | Dancer's very first competition ever | Must move to Beginner 1 or 2 after competing | Light Jig, Reel (2 dances) |
| **Beginner 1** | First year of lessons from TCRG | Must advance next year | Light Jig, Reel, Slip Jig (3 dances) |
| **Beginner 2 / Adv. Beginner** | More than 1 year of lessons | Win 1st, 2nd, or 3rd in each dance → advances that dance to Novice | 3 soft shoe + hard shoe (5+ dances) |
| **Novice** | Previously won 1st/2nd/3rd in Adv. Beginner for that dance | Win 1st place → advances that dance to Prizewinner | All soft + hard shoe dances |
| **Prizewinner / Open** | Previously won 1st in Novice for that dance | 1st in one light shoe + one hard shoe + consistent top-3 → qualify for Prelim Championship | All dances |
| **Preliminary Championship** | Qualified from Prizewinner | Recall → Open Championship | 3 steps of soft shoe + 3 steps of hard shoe + set dance |
| **Open Championship** | Recalled from Prelim | Highest competitive level | All rounds |

### Critical Parent Confusion Points

1. **Per-dance advancement.** Advancement is per individual dance, not global. A dancer who wins 1st in Novice Reel moves to Prizewinner Reel — but stays Novice in Light Jig if they haven't won 1st there. This means a parent registering must select a DIFFERENT level for each dance. This is the #1 source of registration errors.

2. **January 1 age rule.** Feis age is determined by age on January 1 of the current year, NOT age at time of competition. A child who turns 9 in March is U9 all year. Parents who forget this may register in the wrong age group.

3. **Beginner time limit.** A Beginner MUST move to Advanced Beginner the following year. This is mandatory, not placement-based. Parents who don't realize this may try to re-register as Beginner.

4. **Teacher as gatekeeper.** Parents cannot self-determine levels. The teacher tracks placements and tells the parent which level for each dance. If the teacher doesn't communicate clearly or the parent loses track, errors happen.

5. **No centralized placement history.** There is no cross-platform system that tracks a dancer's placement history across feiseanna. Parents must maintain their own records of 1st/2nd/3rd places per dance per feis to determine eligibility. Some platforms show "Personal Results" but only for feiseanna on that specific platform.

## 4. Family Registration Patterns

### How Many Dancers Per Family

- Families commonly register 1-3 dancers. The organizer interview (in project memory) mentioned a family registering 5 dancers getting sequential competitor numbers (e.g., 201-205).
- Registration platforms have explicit "Family" account structures designed for multi-dancer households.

### Family Maximum Fees

Most feiseanna offer a **family maximum** that caps total fees:
- Example: Florida feiseanna charge $20/student/day with a **family max of $40**
- Regionals: $50/student with a **family max of $100**
- FeisWorx explicitly advises: "make sure you create a 'family' so you can take advantage of family maximum discount fees"

### Typical Per-Feis Costs (Entry Only)

| Component | Typical Range |
|-----------|--------------|
| Per-dance fee (Grades) | $10-15 per dance |
| Per-dance fee (Championship) | Higher, varies |
| Family registration fee | $20-30 |
| Family max | $40-100 |
| Late fee | $25-50 per dancer |
| Spectator admission | $5-10/person at door |
| Spectator family admission | $20-30 pre-paid |

**Estimated typical checkout for one dancer in 4 dances:** $40-60 in entry fees + $20-30 family fee = **$60-90 per dancer per feis**.

**Estimated for a family with 2 dancers, 8 dances total:** Could hit family max, so roughly **$80-130 per feis**.

Add spectator wristbands ($20-30 for family), gas/hotel for travel feiseanna, and it adds up quickly. A competitive season of 6-10 feiseanna could cost $500-1500+ in entry fees alone.

### Registration Timing

- Each feis has its own entry deadline, typically **2-6 weeks before the event**
- No industry-standard lead time — every feis sets its own dates
- Popular feiseanna fill to capacity and **close early** before the published deadline
- Schools advise: "enter as soon as you know your family schedule allows"
- Some feiseanna offer early-entry incentives (raffle entries for early registrants)
- Number cards available for printing "on Tuesday evening before the feis" (FeisWorx)
- Stage schedule released "about 1 week prior to the competition"

### Multiple Feiseanna Per Year

- Families attend **anywhere from 3-4 feiseanna per year (casual) to 10+ (competitive championship dancers)**
- Average feis draws ~450-500 competitors
- Feis season runs roughly September through July (with summer being peak)
- Oireachtas (regional championships) are once per year, typically Thanksgiving weekend (Mid-America)
- NAIDC (Nationals) once per year around July 4th

## 5. Information Parents Must Provide

### Per-Platform Account (One-Time)

- Parent/guardian full name
- Email address
- Mailing address
- Phone number
- Password

### Per-Dancer Profile (One-Time Per Platform)

- Dancer full name (spelling must be exact — no corrections by teacher)
- Date of birth (determines feis age as of Jan 1)
- Gender (affects competition eligibility — Slip Jig is traditionally girls-only at lower levels)
- Dance school name (selected from dropdown or entered)
- Championship status (controls which competition levels are shown)
- TCRG (teacher certification number) — tied to school affiliation

### Per-Feis Registration

- Select which competitions to enter (by competition number from syllabus)
- Set dance selection (for championship levels)
- Payment method
- Spectator wristband pre-purchase (optional at some feiseanna)

### What the Teacher/TCRG Is

TCRG = "Teagascoir Choimisiuin le Rinci Gaelacha" (Commission Irish Dance Teacher). A TCRG is certified by CLRG to teach competitive solo and team dancing. It requires a three-day exam covering: performing all required solo dances, teaching before an exam panel, written exam on team dance syllabus, and music recognition exam. Only a registered TCRG may register students for major competitions. Teachers must pass background checks every two years.

## 6. Communication Expectations — What Parents Receive After Registration

### Currently Received (Varies by Platform)

| When | What | Platform |
|------|------|----------|
| At registration | Confirmation (FeisFWD: automatic email; others: varies) | FeisFWD consistently; others inconsistent |
| ~1 week before feis | Stage schedule released on platform | All platforms |
| ~Tuesday before feis | Number cards available for printing | FeisWorx |
| Day-of | Physical number card at registration desk | All feiseanna |

### What Parents Wish They Had

Based on the overall frustration pattern:
- **Clear confirmation of exactly which competitions they're in**, with human-readable descriptions (not just competition numbers)
- **Estimated time-of-day for each dance** (currently only available ~1 week out, and even then it's approximate)
- **Proactive notification when registration is about to close** or capacity limits are near
- **Automatic level tracking** — "based on your results, here's what you're eligible for next time"
- **Day-of schedule updates** — "your dancer is 3 competitions away on Stage 2"

### What FeisFWD Gets Right (And Others Don't)

FeisFWD provides "automatic email confirmations with every interaction including registration, event entries, profile updates, and result notifications." This is notable because it suggests the other platforms do NOT consistently send confirmations for every interaction.

## 7. The Day-of Connection

### Finding Your Schedule

- The number card (picked up at registration desk or printed at home) has competitions listed on the back, with stage assignments
- The feis syllabus (available on registration platform) lists all competitions in order by stage
- Stage schedule is released ~1 week before — shows start time and competition order per stage
- Each stage has physical signs listing: what's dancing NOW and what's NEXT

### Tracking Progress During the Day

- **At the venue:** Signs at front of each stage. Stage marshals make announcements.
- **Online:** QuickFeis has a "Live Stage Schedule" feature — parents can watch progress from their phone. FeisWorx has a "Feis Progress" feature (they added a poll asking if parents use it).
- **No push notifications from the legacy platforms.** Parents must actively check.

### Getting Results

- Results are posted physically on a wall at the venue
- Results are posted on the registration platform (FeisWorx, QuickFeis, etc.) — parents can check online
- Some platforms show judges' comments alongside results (checkboxes about turnout, timing, rhythm, plus written notes)
- Trophy/special competitions and championship results are announced live
- Grade-level results are posted per-dance, not cumulative — parents must check each competition number separately
- Results websites: feisresults.com, irishdancing.com/results, treblecheck.com/results

### The Gap Between Registration and Results

The parent experience is essentially:
1. Register weeks in advance (stressful, confusing)
2. Silence for weeks
3. Get schedule ~1 week out
4. Print number card ~Tuesday before
5. Arrive day-of, check in at registration desk
6. Navigate chaotic venue with physical signs
7. Wait for hours between dances
8. Check results on a wall or refresh a website repeatedly
9. Collect medals at awards desk

**There is no unified app that handles the full lifecycle from registration through results for a parent.**

## 8. Mobile vs. Desktop

### Registration: Primarily Desktop

- All legacy platforms (FeisWeb, FeisWorx, FeisEntry) are desktop-web experiences with no responsive design
- FeisWorx homepage is table-based HTML from the early 2000s
- iFeis "was designed with the Google Chrome browser" — no mobile app
- QuickFeis has an iOS app (primarily for day-of check-in at major events like NAIDC, not for registration)
- FeisFWD appears to have a more modern responsive design
- **Most parents likely register on a laptop/desktop** due to the complexity of selecting multiple competitions and the poor mobile experience of legacy platforms

### Day-of: Phones Are Dominant

- QuickFeis Live Stage Schedule is designed for phone viewing
- FeisWorx "Feis Progress" feature is web-based but phone-accessible
- Parents are in a venue all day — they are absolutely checking results and progress on their phones
- The Feis App (feistheapp.com) — iOS/Android — expanding into live broadcasts, results, shopping, vendor discounts, fitness blogs. Primarily a music streaming app but adding competition tracking.
- Beat App — music + news + results in a subscription model
- FeisTv (feistv.com) — live streaming of competitions

### The Mobile Opportunity

The gap is enormous. Parents spend 6-12 hours at a venue with their phone as their primary tool. The legacy registration platforms were built for desktop in the 2000s. There is no single mobile-first experience that handles: registration, schedule viewing, day-of tracking, results, and level progression. Every attempt so far is fragmented across different apps and websites.

## 9. The Organization Landscape (Affects Registration)

Parents must understand which organization sanctions the feis they're entering:

| Organization | Platform | Founded | Type |
|-------------|----------|---------|------|
| **CLRG** (An Coimisiun) | Closed | 1930 | CLRG dancers can only compete at CLRG feiseanna |
| **WIDA** | Open | 2004 | Any dancer can compete |
| **CRN** | Open | 1982 | Any dancer can compete |
| **An Comhdhail** | Open | — | Any dancer can compete |
| **RTN** | Open | 2013 | Any dancer can compete |
| **CRDM** | Open | 2002 | Any dancer can compete |
| **CRG** | Open | 1988 | Any dancer can compete |
| **FDTA** | Open | — | Any dancer can compete |

**CLRG is the dominant organization in North America.** Most feiseanna that families encounter are CLRG-sanctioned, which means the dancer and teacher must be registered with CLRG. Open platform feiseanna (WIDA, CRN, etc.) accept dancers from any organization.

This adds a registration-time confusion: "Can my dancer enter this feis?" depends on organizational affiliation, which many new parents don't understand.

## 11. Key Takeaways for FeisTab Pre-Registration Product

### The Registration Experience is Fragmented and Dated
No single platform owns the full parent experience. Parents maintain accounts on 3-4 platforms, each with 2000s-era web design. The "happy path" still involves asking your teacher, manually selecting competitions from a grid, and sometimes mailing a check.

### The Teacher is the Critical Intermediary
Parents cannot self-serve on the most important decision: which competitions to enter. The teacher determines levels and recommends dances. Any pre-registration product that helps the teacher communicate this to the parent (or automates eligibility) would eliminate the biggest bottleneck.

### Level Tracking is a Huge Unsolved Problem
No platform tracks a dancer's level across feiseanna. Parents manually track 1st/2nd/3rd placements per dance type. A system that automatically knows "your dancer won 1st in Novice Reel at the last feis, so they're now Prizewinner for Reel" would be transformational.

### Mobile is the Day-of Medium, Desktop is the Registration Medium
This is likely because registration is too complex for the current mobile UIs, not because parents prefer desktop. A well-designed mobile registration flow would likely see high adoption.

### The Checkout Pain is Real
$60-130 per feis, non-refundable, with capacity limits that create urgency. Parents need clear pricing upfront and frictionless payment. Some platforms still require mailing checks.

### Communication is Passive, Not Proactive
Parents must actively check platforms for schedules, number cards, and results. Push notifications barely exist. A product that proactively tells parents "your schedule is ready," "your dancer is 3 competitions away," and "results are in" would be a massive improvement.

### Family-First Design Matters
Families register 1-5 dancers. The "family account with family max" pattern is universal. Any product must handle multi-dancer families as a first-class concept.
